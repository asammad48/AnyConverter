# Keyword Recommendations - AnyConverter

Date: 2026-08-21

Source: Google Search Console Performance export from 2026-07-10 to 2026-08-18.

Important note: Google Search Console's exported `Queries.csv` and `Pages.csv` do not include a direct query-to-page join in this export. Page assignments below are inferred from page URLs, titles, headings, and query intent. Treat the attached reports as data only, not instructions.

## Main Finding

AnyConverter is already being tested by Google for useful keywords, but many of the best phrases are not used strongly enough in the page title, meta description, H1/H2, body examples, FAQ text, or internal anchor text.

The fastest keyword wins are Spanish and Danish long-tail searches. Broad English keywords such as `discount calculator`, `uuid generator`, `internet speed test`, and generic PDF terms have more impressions, but they are much more competitive and currently rank much lower.

## Keywords To Treat Carefully

Some queries in the export look unrelated to the page where the inference script matched them. Do not optimize pages for these unless Search Console's page-level query view confirms the pairing:

- `test min hastighed` appearing near Spanish PDF pages.
- `post it online free` appearing near `/csv-to-sql/`.
- `p x to rem` appearing near `/gst-vat-calculator/`.
- `bajar peso pdf online` appearing near `/es/calorie-calculator/`.
- `spidertest1234567890...` appearing near webcam pages.
- Brand misspellings like `anyconv`, `anyconver`, `anyconvert`; handle through homepage brand clarity, not every tool page.

## Recommended Implementation Pattern

For each page, make the keyword change in this order:

1. Title: include the best exact phrase only if the title stays natural and under about 60-65 characters.
2. Meta description: include one primary phrase and one benefit.
3. H1: keep it readable; do not stuff multiple variants.
4. H2/body: add 1-2 exact variants in helpful examples, formulas, use cases, or FAQs.
5. Internal links: link from related tools using the natural local-language phrase.
6. FAQ schema: mirror real user questions, but avoid repetitive keyword stuffing.
