How the ascendant is calculated

Your rising sign, or ascendant, is the zodiac sign that was rising on the eastern horizon at the moment and place of your birth. To find it, the calculator converts your birth time to Universal Time, works out the local sidereal time for your longitude, and combines it with your latitude and the tilt of the Earth’s axis to find the point of the zodiac on the horizon.

Because the Earth turns once a day, a new sign rises roughly every two hours, and the ascendant degree moves about one degree every four minutes. That is why this calculator does not guess when the time is missing, and why it shows whether your sign would change if your recorded time were 15 minutes off. Above about 66° latitude some signs rise very quickly or not at all, and results become unreliable.

The positions are calculated with standard astronomical formulas in the tropical zodiac. The meaning of a rising sign belongs to astrology and is not scientifically established. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a rising sign? +
The zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the time and place of your birth.
Is rising sign the same as ascendant? +
Yes. Both names describe the same point.
Is birth time required? +
Yes. Without an exact time the ascendant can be any of the twelve signs, so the calculator asks for it.
Can I calculate without birth time? +
Not reliably. Use the Moon Sign Calculator, which handles an unknown time, and try to find your time on your birth certificate or from family.
Why does location matter? +
The ascendant depends on your local horizon: both your latitude and longitude change the result.
Why did another site give a different sign? +
Check the time, time zone and daylight saving setting, and whether the site uses the tropical or sidereal zodiac. This calculator uses tropical.
Does it store birth details? +
No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is stored or sent.
Is ascendant interpretation scientifically proven? +
No. Treat the interpretation as astrology, not established psychology.
