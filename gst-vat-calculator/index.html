Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between GST and VAT? +
GST (Goods and Services Tax) and VAT (Value Added Tax) are both consumption taxes levied on the value added at each stage of the supply chain. The terminology varies primarily by jurisdiction: Canada, Australia, and New Zealand use GST, while the UK and European Union use VAT.
How do I calculate and remove GST from a gross total price? +
To remove GST from a gross amount, divide the total price by (1 + tax rate / 100). For example, with a 10% GST rate on a $110 purchase: 110 ÷ 1.10 = $100 net price, meaning $10 was GST.
How do I add GST or VAT to a net price? +
Multiply the net pre-tax price by the tax rate percentage to calculate the tax amount, then add it to the net price. For example, on a $200 purchase with a 20% VAT rate: 200 × 0.20 = $40 tax, giving a gross total of $240.
What are standard VAT and GST rates in the UK, Australia, and Canada? +
The standard UK VAT rate is 20% (with a 5% reduced rate for select goods). Australia levies a flat 10% GST. Canada applies a 5% federal GST, which combines with provincial taxes in HST provinces (e.g., 13% in Ontario, 15% in Atlantic provinces).
Can this calculator be used for invoicing and tax returns? +
Yes. You can switch between "Add Tax" and "Remove Tax" modes to determine accurate pre-tax subtotals, exact tax liabilities, and gross invoice amounts for business accounting and tax filings.
