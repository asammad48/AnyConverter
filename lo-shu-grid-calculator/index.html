How the Lo Shu Grid works

The Lo Shu Grid is a 3×3 magic square from Chinese tradition in which every row, column and diagonal adds up to 15. The numbers sit in fixed places: 4 9 2 on top, 3 5 7 in the middle and 8 1 6 at the bottom. In numerology, each digit of your birth date is placed in its cell, so a date with three 1s shows 111 in the bottom middle.

Empty cells are your missing numbers, and repeated digits are said to strengthen that quality. Lines matter too: a full row, column or diagonal is called an arrow of strength, and a completely empty one an arrow of weakness. Example for 15 July 1990: digits 1, 5, 7, 1, 9, 9 give 11 in the 1 cell, 99 in the 9 cell, and the Will plane (9–5–1) complete.

In the Vedic or Indian method, the Driver number (your day of birth reduced) and the Destiny number (the whole date reduced) are added to the grid as well. This is a symbolic tradition, not a science. The date stays in your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Lo Shu Grid? +
A 3×3 number grid based on the Lo Shu magic square, used in modern numerology to map the digits of your birth date.
What data do I need? +
Only your birth date. No time, place or name is required for the core grid.
What do repeated numbers mean? +
A number that appears more than once is traditionally read as a stronger, sometimes excessive, version of that quality. The count is shown in each cell.
What are missing numbers? +
Digits from 1 to 9 that do not appear in your birth date. Tradition links each to a quality to develop.
What are arrows or planes? +
The eight lines of the grid. A complete line is an arrow of strength; an empty line is an arrow of weakness.
Is birth time required? +
No. The standard grid uses only the date.
Can I download the grid? +
Yes. Download it as a PNG image. It is created in your browser and nothing is uploaded.
Is Lo Shu numerology scientific? +
No. It is a traditional interpretive system. Use it for reflection and fun.
