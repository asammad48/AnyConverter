Frequently Asked Questions

What is considered a good download and upload speed? +
For general web browsing and HD streaming, 25 Mbps download is sufficient. For 4K streaming, remote work, and video conferencing, 50 to 100 Mbps is recommended. Competitive gaming and large file uploads benefit from 100+ Mbps download and 20+ Mbps upload.
What is the difference between download speed, upload speed, and ping? +
Download speed measures how fast data transfers from the internet to your device. Upload speed measures how quickly you send data to others. Ping (latency) measures the round-trip reaction time in milliseconds; lower ping means a more responsive connection.
Why does my speed test result differ from my ISP's advertised speed? +
Advertised speeds represent theoretical maximums under optimal conditions. Real-world speeds can be affected by Wi-Fi interference, distance from the router, network congestion during peak hours, background downloads, and router hardware limits.
How can I improve my internet speed test results? +
Connect your computer directly via an Ethernet cable rather than Wi-Fi, close bandwidth-heavy streaming or cloud sync apps, restart your modem and router, and run tests at different times of day to rule out ISP throttling.
Does running this speed test upload or log personal data? +
No. The speed test transmits temporary dummy data chunks solely to calculate bandwidth throughput and round-trip latency. No personal browsing data, IP logs, or files are collected or stored.
