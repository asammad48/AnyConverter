How to play FLAMES

  1. Type two names. Full names or first names both work. Spaces, capitals and accents are ignored.
  2. Watch the letters cancel. Letters the names share are crossed out in pairs. The letters left over are counted.
  3. Count around FLAMES. Count around the word FLAMES with that number, removing a letter each time, until one is left.

How FLAMES is calculated

FLAMES stands for Friends, Love, Affection, Marriage, Enemies and Siblings. Write both names, cross out every letter they have in common (one for one, so two A’s in one name only cancel two A’s in the other), and count the letters that remain.

Then count around the letters F-L-A-M-E-S using that number. The letter you land on is removed, and you start counting again from the next letter. Repeat until one letter is left: that is your result. Example: “Ana” and “Leo” share no letters, so 6 remain. Counting 6 removes S, then F, A, L and E in turn, leaving M for Marriage.

FLAMES is a traditional playground game, not relationship science. The names stay in your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does FLAMES stand for? +
Friends, Love, Affection, Marriage, Enemies and Siblings.
How is FLAMES calculated? +
Cancel the letters both names share, count the letters left, and use that number to eliminate letters from FLAMES until one remains.
Are results random? +
No. The same names always give the same result, in either order.
Does capitalization matter? +
No. Upper and lower case are treated the same.
What about spaces or accents? +
Spaces are ignored and accented letters are converted to their base letter, so José and Jose give the same result.
Is FLAMES accurate? +
It is a traditional game, not relationship science.
Can I share the result? +
Yes. Download a share image, or copy a link that shows only your initials and the result.
Are names uploaded? +
No. Everything runs in your browser.
