How to Use the Discount Stacking Calculator

  1. Enter the price, quantity, and shipping, and choose whether tax is included in the price, added at checkout, or not applicable.
  2. Add each discount in the order your store applies them — percent off, a fixed coupon, a minimum-spend offer, a multi-buy deal, or cashback.
  3. Read the total: what you pay, the real discount percentage, the "stacking gap" versus the advertised sum, and whether a different order would save more.

Features

  • 11 discount step types: percent off, fixed amount, capped percent, minimum-spend thresholds, price overrides, multi-buy (buy X get Y free, Nth item off), quantity discounts, and cashback
  • Shows the "stacking gap" — the difference between the advertised sum of discounts and what you actually save
  • Order analysis: tells you whether your order is already the best, or how much a different order would save, for the discounts where order matters
  • VAT-included pricing (Denmark, Spain) or sales-tax-added pricing (US-style), plus a "doesn't reduce taxable amount" flag for manufacturer coupons
  • Cashback shown separately from the checkout price, since it's paid back later, not deducted at checkout

  • "Find the extra % needed" to reach a target price, and a "copy summary" button for sharing the result
  • Runs entirely in your browser — no signup, no data sent anywhere

Why 20% + 10% isn't 30% off

Stacked percent discounts multiply, they don't add. Each discount applies to the already-reduced price: final = price × (1 − d₁) × (1 − d₂). A 20% discount followed by 10% gives 1 − (0.8 × 0.9) = 28% off, not 30%. On an 800 TV with 40% off plus an extra 10%, you pay 432, not 400 — a 32 "stacking gap" between what the sign implies and what you actually save.

When the order of discounts matters

With percent-only discounts, order never changes the result. It starts to matter once you mix in fixed amounts, minimum-spend coupons, price caps, or multi-buy offers. A 55 cart with a 10% discount and a "10 off orders over 50" coupon: apply the threshold coupon first and you pay 40.50; apply the percent first and the subtotal drops to 49.50 — below the 50 threshold, so the coupon no longer qualifies, and you pay 49.50 instead.

VAT included vs. sales tax added

In Denmark and Spain, VAT is already inside the shelf price, so a discount reduces the VAT too — nothing is added at the till. In the US, sales tax is normally added on top of the discounted price at checkout, though some states tax manufacturer coupons on the pre-coupon amount.

Multi-buy offers and cashback

"3 for 2" and "buy X get Y free" offers make the cheapest qualifying units free; "cashback" isn't a checkout discount at all — you pay the full discounted price and the money comes back later, so it's shown as a separate "net cost after cashback."

Successive discounts: the formula

Successive discounts are applied one after another, each to the price left by the previous one. The single equivalent discount is 1 − (1 − d₁)(1 − d₂)…(1 − dₙ). For 20%, 10% and 5%: 1 − 0.8 × 0.9 × 0.95 = 31.6%, not 35%. Use the quick successive discount calculator above the results for percent-only chains, or add each step to the full calculator to mix in coupons, thresholds and cashback.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate successive discounts? +
Multiply the remaining fractions and subtract from 1: 1 − (1 − d₁)(1 − d₂)… Successive discounts of 20% and 10% equal one 28% discount.
Is 20% off plus 10% off the same as 30% off? +
No. The second discount applies to the already reduced price, so the total is 28% off, not 30%.
Does the order of discounts matter? +
Not for percent-only discounts. It matters when you mix percentages with fixed amounts, minimum-spend coupons, caps, or multi-buy offers. The calculator shows the best and worst order.
Is tax calculated before or after discounts? +
Sales tax is normally charged on the discounted price. In Europe, VAT is already included in the shelf price, so discounts reduce the VAT too.
How do I calculate "buy 2 get 1 free"? +
Add a "Buy X get Y free" step with X = 2 and Y = 1, and set the quantity. Every third unit becomes free.
Is cashback a discount? +
Not at checkout. You pay the full discounted price and get money back later, so it is shown separately as net cost after cashback.
Will the store accept my combination of discounts? +
The calculator only does the maths. Check the coupon terms, since many stores limit which discounts can be combined.
